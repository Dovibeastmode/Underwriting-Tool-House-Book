## Standing box rules (as applied this session)

- Factor rates: **1.40 / 1.45 / 1.49 only**. 1.35 is the prepay rate, not a pricing tier. 1.50+ is a hard no.
- Underwriting fee 5% of funded, deducted from disbursement.
- Positions: fund up to a 4th; 5th+ existing = decline (tunable).
- Daily payment counts divisible by 5; weekly counts any integer.
- Max term 9 months / ~195 collection days.
- User checks court records himself. Always stip **DataMerch, UCC search, positions/payoff letters**.
- Preferred industries: restaurants, home-care / healthcare / behavioral health.
- Standing output: monthly true-revenue table + 5-box scorecard + positions story/funder table.

### Conversion math (the rule that keeps coming up)
- **Money** converts with **5** and **21.655**: weekly ÷ 5 = daily; monthly ÷ 21.655 = daily.
- **Time** converts with **4.331**: weeks ÷ 4.331 = months.
- Cross-check: weekly ÷ 5 × 21.655 = weekly × 4.331. Same answer both routes.

### 5. BJ's Dawg Gone Good BBQ LLC — restaurant, Athens GA (Truist)
**Method note: how to get balances when the bank doesn't print them.**

Truist gives previous balance + new balance on page 1 but no daily table. Rebuild it:
1. Start at the beginning balance.
2. Walk transactions in date order — deposits add, withdrawals/checks subtract, carry forward daily.
3. Reconciled walk must equal the printed new balance. If not, you missed lines (usually checks).
4. Then read avg balance, low day, negative days off your curve.
5. Cheap anchors without the full walk: statement-to-statement continuity, and the ending balances themselves.

### 14. Intuit Financing / QuickBooks Capital — is it a position?
**Yes it's real debt; no it's not an MCA position.**

- "Intuit Financing / QBC_PMTS" = QuickBooks Capital, a line of credit issued through **WebBank**. Tell on the deposit side: **"WEBBANK/INTUIT … LOAN"** credits (seen on GlobalPoint: $20,500 and $28,000 in).
- **Why amounts look random:** each draw amortizes as its own installment. Three draws in different months at different sizes = three fixed schedules overlapping. Small figures ($2,838.84, $2,461.40) are per-draw monthly payments; large ones ($16,143.84, $24,212.08) are payoffs or lump principal paydowns. If a figure never recurs ~30 days later, it's Intuit's payroll-funding product instead.
- **Treatment:** do NOT count toward the 4th-position cutoff — it's bank-style credit, not a receivables purchase, and creates no stacking/breach dynamic. DO count the monthly total as leverage when sizing the payment. And treat the limit as the exposure — he can redraw the day after you fund.

### Fix 1 — MoneyBadger cross-check was wrong on daily deals

**Cause:** `L7` used `D8*4/C24` — weekly payment × 4. Correct for weekly, wrong for daily.

**Rule established:** MoneyBadger uses **4 weeks/month on weekly deals** and **21 business days/month on daily deals**. Our tool uses 4.331 and 21.655 for both.

**New formulas:**
```
J6 = IF($E$6="Weekly","Weekly remittance","Daily remittance")
K6 = IF(OR($E$6="",C8="",C24=0),"",IF($E$6="Weekly",D8,C8))
L6 = IF(OR(L7="",C24=0),"",L7*C24/IF($E$6="Weekly",$L$8,$L$9))
L7 = IF(OR($E$6="",C8="",C24=0),"",ROUND(IF($E$6="Weekly",D8*$L$8,C8*$L$9)/C24*100,2)/100)
```
Constants as editable cells: **L8 = 4** (weeks/month), **L9 = 21** (business days/month), with our own K8 = 4.331 / K9 = 21.655 beside them. O5 caveat deleted.

That's why the displayed remittance is $507.62 rather than the $507.50 actually collected — M6 shows MB's rounding artifact, which is the useful signal.

## Recurring lessons from this session

1. **Always pull the sibling accounts.** OnDeck hid on Kaushik's Leader account; AdvanceServicing moved from checking to savings; Fundation repays from an unseen account; Ergode has 6+ unseen accounts. A position is not closed until you've checked every account.
2. **Processor-funded advances leave no repayment trail.** DoorDash Capital, Square Capital, Shopify/Stripe Capital withhold at source — the only footprint is the funding deposit and suppressed payouts.
3. **Gross deposits lie.** Owner injections, inter-company cycling, and funding deposits routinely make up 25–45% of "revenue."
4. **Low days beat average balances.** Several files showed healthy averages and near-zero lows on payroll or sweep days — that's what actually bounces a fixed pull.
5. **Third-party servicer names hide the funder.** "AdvanceServicing" is a platform, not a lender. UCC search surfaces the real name.
